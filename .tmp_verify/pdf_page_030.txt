             2.0 Introduction
             [CATEGORY A — ESTABLISHED]
             Picture a garden hose stretched across a backyard. Seen from a second-story
             window, the hose looks like a thin one-dimensional line—an ant crawling
             along it appears restricted solely to moving forward or backward. Step closer
             with a magnifying glass, however, and you see that an ant tiny enough can
             also walk all the way around the tight circular thickness of the hose.
             Beginning with Theodor Kaluza in 1921 and Oskar Klein in 1926, theoretical
             physicists have used that intuitive geometry to ask a profound structural
             question: could our familiar four-dimensional universe hide an ultra-small
             circular fifth direction at every point, or sit as a suspended four-dimensional
             membrane inside a warped higher-dimensional space?
