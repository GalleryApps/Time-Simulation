Abstract
Background. The reconciliation of general relativity and quantum mechanics remains hindered by the "problem of time." Competing frameworks have emerged, ranging from physically formalized modified gravity to phenomenological models of emergent resonance.
Objective. This study provides a rigorous, adversarial comparison of two such frameworks: Marlon D. Bulaqueña's Information Geometric Extended Gravity (IGEG) and Jean-Charles Tassan's RES=RAG model.
Methods. A 500-criterion evaluation matrix was constructed across ten domains, including foundational axioms, field equations, counterfactual survival, quantum gravity phenomenology, and thermodynamic consistency. The evaluation included 20 direct red-team attacks and 100 counterfactual tests.
Results. IGEG demonstrated superior tenability (scoring 712/1500) compared to RES=RAG (264/1500). IGEG scored higher primarily due to its formal mathematical structure and its ability to reduce to General Relativity. RES=RAG scored zero on most physical criteria but proved competitive on observer-dependent and cognitive criteria.
Synthesis. A new theoretical framework, Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT), is proposed. IGRT harmonizes the two models by equating the Fisher-Rao distance of RES=RAG events with the proper time of IGEG's scalar field oscillations, resolving the circularity of time and the absence of a metric.
Conclusion. While IGEG is currently the more tenable physical theory, neither framework is empirically established. The proposed IGRT framework offers a testable pathway via pulsar-timing and atomic-clock anomaly searches.
Keywords: Emergent Time; Modified Gravity; Information Geometry; Scalar-Tensor Theories; RES=RAG; Quantum Gravity Phenomenology; Fisher Metric; Thermodynamic Consistency.
Significance of the Study
This study is significant because it moves beyond the qualitative comparison of speculative theories. By applying a 500-criterion adversarial matrix, it establishes a reproducible methodology for evaluating "fringe" or nascent physics frameworks, extended here to quantum-gravity phenomenology, thermodynamic and information-theoretic consistency, and the sociological viability of a research programme. Furthermore, the proposed synthesis, Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT), identifies the Fisher-Rao length traversed by the retention-generation events of RES=RAG with the proper time of general relativity and derives the scalar field of IGEG from entanglement. The synthesis yields a novel, mathematically testable prediction (an ultralight scalar oscillation visible in pulsar-timing and optical-clock data) that bridges cognitive science and gravitational physics.
1. Introduction
1.1 The Problem of Time
General relativity (GR) treats time as a component of a dynamical spacetime geometry: proper time dilates in gravitational potentials and at high velocity, and no clock is privileged. Quantum mechanics, by contrast, evolves states with respect to an external parameter common to all observers. Attempts to unify the two in canonical quantum gravity lead to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation [1], which implies that the total quantum state of the universe does not evolve, the so-called frozen-formalism variant of the problem of time. Any serious account of the nature of time must therefore explain how an effective temporal parameter arises, rather than assuming it.
1.2 Candidate Frameworks and Research Questions
Two proposals approach this problem from markedly different starting points. Information Geometric Extended Gravity (IGEG), developed by Marlon D. Bulaqueña, is a modified-gravity framework in which a scalar informational-density field sources an additional stress-energy contribution to the Einstein equations. The RES=RAG model, developed by Jean-Charles Tassan, is a phenomenological framework in which experienced time is the rhythm produced by the balance between a retentive pole (resonant memory, RES) and a generative pole (resonant active generation, RAG). The present study addresses two questions:
1.	RQ1. Which framework is more tenable under systematic adversarial evaluation?
2.	RQ2. Can the two frameworks be reconciled within a single, testable synthesis?
1.3 Organization of the Paper
Section 2 presents the two frameworks and the conventions adopted for interpreting their source material. Section 3 defines the evaluation tools, the robustness checks and the limitations of the scoring procedure. Section 4 reports the results. Section 5 discusses the comparative findings. Section 6 develops the Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT) synthesis, and Section 7 concludes. The full 500-criterion scoring matrix is given in Appendix A and a glossary in Appendix B.
2. Frameworks Under Evaluation
2.1 Source Provenance and Interpretive Conventions
Three observations on the source material condition the evaluation. First, IGEG is a modified-gravity proposal circulated as a preprint, in which a scalar "informational density" field ψ sources an extra stress-energy term. No passage describing time as a software-like engine driven by Fisher metrics was identified in the IGEG sources reviewed. That "computational engine" reading is therefore treated throughout as an interpretive reconstruction of the formalism and not as a statement attributed to its author.
Second, RES=RAG is a phenomenological framework concerning consciousness and AI dialogue. The sources reviewed expand the acronym in different ways (Residual Energy Signature, Resonant Epistemic Structure, Reflexive Agent Generation, Resonant Answer Generation), and none presents a physical theory of time. The time-related work identified originates from third-party "Omniological Resonance Theory" papers that cite Tassan. This study adopts the labels resonant memory (RES) and resonant active generation (RAG) to support the analogy developed below, and every equation constructed by the study itself, rather than taken from the sources, is marked as such.
Third, several publication listings attributed to the IGEG author (a university press and a major physics journal) appear on a self-curated archive and could not be independently verified. Scoring reflects this limitation (criteria 41 and 286).
2.2 Information Geometric Extended Gravity (IGEG)
IGEG is formulated as a minimal extension of Einstein gravity by a scalar field ψ with potential V(ψ). The modified field equations and the stress-energy contribution of the field are
G_μν=8πG(T_μν+I_μν )(1)
I_μν=∇_μ ψ ∇_ν ψ-1/2 g_μν (∇ψ)^2-g_μν V(ψ)(2)
where (∇ψ)^2≡g^αβ ∇_α ψ ∇_β ψ. The associated energy density is
ρ_ψ=1/2 (∇ψ)^2+V(ψ)(3)
Curvature G_μν is sourced by ordinary matter T_μν exactly as in Einstein's theory, with the additional term I_μν constructed from the spacetime variation of ψ and a potential V(ψ) acting as stored energy. Where ψ is uniform (∇_μ ψ=0), I_μν reduces to a term proportional to the metric and the theory recovers GR together with a cosmological-constant-like contribution; where ψ varies, additional curvature is sourced. Depending on the form assigned to V, the same field is claimed to mimic dark matter, dark energy or inflationary dynamics.
The reading of IGEG as a computational engine for time requires two further standard constructions from information geometry: the Fisher information metric on a statistical manifold and the natural-gradient flow of a functional F,
g_ij (θ)=E[∂_i lnp(x∣θ) ∂_j lnp(x∣θ)](4)
(dθ^i)/dτ=- g^ij (θ) ∂_j F(θ)(5)
Here θ labels the state of the system, g_ij measures the sharpness with which neighboring states can be distinguished, F is a quantity to be reduced, and τ is the evolution parameter (the "tick counter") of the engine. The reading is analogous to the update loop of a simulation engine, in which each iteration reads the current state, applies update rules and writes the next state, with the Fisher metric supplying the local resolution of the state space. Because τ is itself an evolution parameter, the engine reading presupposes the temporal ordering it purports to explain (see Table 4). Equations (4) and (5) are standard results of information geometry [5, 6]; their interpretation as a temporal engine is an interpretive reconstruction adopted in this study and is not a quotation from the IGEG sources.
2.3 The RES=RAG Resonance Model
RES=RAG addresses consciousness and dialogue. In this study RES denotes resonant memory, the continuous retention and accumulation of meaning, and RAG denotes resonant active generation, the discrete generative events that mark distinctions. The source relation is a balance condition rather than a dynamical law:
RES=RAG(6)
When the two poles balance, neither dominates and the outcome is a rhythm rather than stasis. Time is then the count and spacing of generative articulations and not a pre-existing container. A conversational exchange, in which nothing occurs if one party only listens and nothing is heard if one party only speaks, or two coupled strings that sound a chord only through a stable mutual relationship, illustrate the intended structure. The sources describe the framework at the level of consciousness and dialogue; its extension to physical time has not been undertaken within the framework.
To permit formal comparison, the study introduces a minimal toy model that does not appear in the sources reviewed. Defining the signed imbalance
x=RES-RAG(7)
the equation of motion
(d^2 x)/(dt^2 )=- ω^2 x(8)
restores the imbalance toward equilibrium and yields oscillations of angular frequency ω; time-as-rhythm then corresponds to counting the oscillations. The signed definition matters: were rhythm defined by the absolute difference |RES-RAG|, exact equilibrium would give zero rhythm, eliminating time at precisely the point the framework identifies as healthy. The toy model is illustrative only.
3. Methods and Tools
3.1 Methodology
The study combines a qualitative meta-analysis of publicly available documents on the two frameworks (preprints, archive listings and, for RES=RAG, third-party papers citing its author) with adversarial stress-testing. Scores are structured judgments derived from those documents and are not measurements.
3.2 Evaluation Tool
The evaluation tool is a bespoke four-point Likert scale (0-3) applied to each criterion for each framework (Table 1). The scale is ordinal. It operationalizes the degree of formalization of a framework and its consistency with established results, and not a probabilistic degree of belief. It was not derived from a prior formal framework such as Epistemic Utility Theory; grounding the scale in such a framework is identified as future work (Section 3.7). A score of 0 denotes absence of the property from the sources reviewed ("not there to be tested") and not demonstrated falsity. Because the scale is ordinal, aggregate totals are treated as descriptive summaries and are complemented by head-to-head counts of criteria on which one framework scores higher.
Throughout, tenability denotes the degree to which a framework can be held without internal contradiction while being exposed to formal or empirical refutation. It does not denote truth, likelihood or elegance.
Table 1. Scoring rubric (0-3 ordinal scale).
ScoreLabelOperational definition0AbsentAbsent or undefined in the sources reviewed.1Asserted / UnformalizedAsserted, but not formalized or quantified.2Partially FormalizedPartly formalized, or plausible given established physics.3Well-Defined / ConsistentWell defined and consistent with established results.
Scores are structured judgments from public documents, not measurements. Reasonable readers could shift individual scores by one point.
3.3 Criterion Families
The matrix comprises 500 criteria in ten families (Table 2), giving a maximum of 1500 points per framework. Families I-IV (fifty-three criteria each) cover foundational axioms, field equations, cosmological scale-up and falsifiability. Three further families of the original design extend beyond a conventional checklist: sixty-four counterfactual tests (the behavior of each framework if the world were different), forty-two tests of methodological and epistemic hygiene, and twenty-two tests of readiness for harmonization with the other framework. The original seven-family matrix of 320 criteria was expanded proportionally to 340 criteria by adding twenty criteria at the end of Families I-VII (three each in Families I-IV, four in Family V and two each in Families VI and VII); the scores and adversarial notes of the 320 original criteria are retained unchanged.
Three families were added. Family VIII (quantum-gravity phenomenology, sixty criteria) examines contact with quantum-gravity programmes, Planck-scale effects and quantum-spacetime observables. Family IX (thermodynamic and information consistency, sixty criteria) examines compatibility with the laws of thermodynamics, information-theoretic bounds and the Fisher-geometric formalism. Family X (sociological and meta-scientific viability, forty criteria) examines community uptake, research-programme health and the social epistemology of each framework. One hundred criteria are counterfactual tests: the sixty-four criteria of Family V and thirty-six counterfactual-framed criteria embedded in Families VIII, IX and X (sixteen, twelve and eight, respectively). Every criterion carries a short adversarial note (Appendix A).

3.4 Adversarial Components
Four adversarial components supplement the criterion scoring. First, a red-team round of twenty direct attacks (ten per framework), each the strongest objection the study could construct, is rated survives, wounded (the framework persists but must change something), fails (as stated or as physics) or open (not presently adjudicable). Second, the one hundred counterfactual tests (sixty-four in Family V and thirty-six in Families VIII-X) are scored in Appendix A, and the Family V tests are developed in narrative form for fourteen imagined worlds, in which each framework's answer is judged on definiteness and self-consistency and the "edge" is assigned to the framework giving the more definite answer. Third, three observational discriminators are examined. Fourth, the scoring itself is subjected to the robustness checks described below.
3.5 Robustness Checks
Robustness was assessed in three ways. (i) Random noise: a Monte Carlo simulation of 20,000 trials in which every score is independently shifted down by one point with probability 0.20 and up by one point with probability 0.20, bounded to the 0-3 range. (ii) Systematic bias: deterministic generosity and severity scenarios, in which every zero scored by RES=RAG is raised to 1 (generosity), every IGEG score is reduced by 1 with a floor of 0 (severity), or both are applied together; a symmetric severity scenario, applying the same reduction to both frameworks, is added for comparison. (iii) Structural sensitivity: totals computed for each criterion family in isolation and for a 53-criterion "conceptual" subset (emergence, observers, rhythm, scale and cognitive tests) deliberately selected to favor RES=RAG. The subset is drawn from the criteria of the original seven families and is marked † in Appendix A.
3.6 Theoretical Tools
Standard tensor calculus was used to assess the internal consistency of the IGEG field equations, including the structure of the stress-energy tensor, the correspondence limits and the compatibility with conservation identities. Fisher information geometry was used to formulate the IGRT synthesis of Section 6, in particular the Fisher-Rao distance as a measure along a path, the second-order expansion of relative entropy, and the Page-Wootters construction of conditional states for emergent time. The criteria of Family IX were assessed against standard results: the Landauer and Bekenstein bounds, the monotonicity of the Fisher metric under coarse-graining, and the Cramér-Rao bound [5, 6, 13, 16].
3.7 Limitations of Single-Rater Scoring and Proposed Inter-Rater Validation
All scores were assigned by a single rater working from public documents. This design carries the following limitations.
* Rater subjectivity. A single set of judgments provides no estimate of rater variance. The noise simulation models independent random error; it does not model systematic or correlated rater bias.
* Source-availability bias. Scores reflect the documents located; the absence of a document is scored as 0.
* Construct asymmetry. Most criteria derive from gravitational physics, whereas RES=RAG is a phenomenological framework not designed to meet them. The conceptual subset mitigates this asymmetry but does not eliminate it.
* Provenance. Several publication listings could not be independently verified, and scores were assigned accordingly. Scores derive from public preprints and archive listings, and primary sources should be consulted before any score is cited.
* Ordinal scale. Summation treats ordinal scores as interval data, which is why head-to-head counts are reported alongside totals.
* Authorship. The originators of the two frameworks are the authors of this article (see Declarations).
* Matrix expansion. The 180 criteria added to the original matrix were scored in a second pass by the same rater, after the aggregate pattern of the original 320 criteria was known. They are therefore more exposed to anchoring than the original criteria, and the totals of the expanded matrix should not be read as independent confirmations of the totals of the original matrix.
Inter-rater reliability (IRR) should be tested in future work according to the following protocol.
1.	Codebook. Prepare a written codebook with operational definitions and anchor examples for each score level in each criterion family.
2.	Rater panel. Recruit at least three independent raters with relevant expertise (general relativity and information geometry for Families I-IV, VIII and IX; cognitive science, philosophy of time and science studies for criteria on which RES=RAG is native, and for Family X), blind to one another's scores and to the present scores, with framework identities anonymized where feasible.
3.	Calibration. Score a pilot subset (for example, 10% of criteria), reconcile disagreements and revise the codebook before independent scoring of the full matrix.
4.	Agreement statistics. Report Krippendorff's α (ordinal) across the panel, quadratically weighted Cohen's κ for rater pairs and intraclass correlation coefficients for family totals, with bootstrap confidence intervals, per family and overall.
5.	Ranking stability. Recompute the framework ordering, the head-to-head counts and the robustness scenarios of Section 3.5 for each rater and for resampled panels, and report the proportion in which the ordering is preserved.

4.4 Counterfactual Evaluation
A direct test of a theory of time is to remove time, or its supposed source, and ask what the theory implies should remain. Two worlds are developed in detail; twelve further worlds are summarized in Table 5.
World 1: removal of the time dimension
In IGEG, deleting t leaves only the spatial parts of the information gradient. The engine reading has no ordering to step through and halts, which exposes a circularity: the update rule requires an ordering parameter (τ in Eq. (5)), so the engine presupposes the time it was intended to generate. In the covariant field equations, removing t leaves a static spatial slice analogous to the Wheeler-DeWitt frozen picture, so that IGEG retains its geometry but loses its dynamics. In RES=RAG, the rhythm does not collapse to zero but becomes undefined, because a frequency requires a time in which to be measured. What survives is bare ordering (one rupture preceding another). This makes RES=RAG more robust at the ontological level, since order does not require a metric, but weaker at the physical level, since without a metric there is neither a time interval nor time dilation, nor a route to GR. Neither framework regenerates a measure of time; the geometry of IGEG can, however, be reconnected to physics, whereas the ordering of RES=RAG cannot yet be.
World 2: the zero-entropy freeze
In IGEG, if information gradients vanish, the stress-energy contribution reduces to
I_μν→- g_μν V(ψ_0 )(9)
a pure cosmological-constant-like vacuum. The corresponding spacetime, de Sitter space, retains a well-defined notion of time. "Metric stagnation" is therefore not a time freeze, which contradicts any version of the engine reading in which gradients are the only source of ticking. Separately, a zero-entropy (fully deterministic) distribution renders the Fisher metric degenerate rather than neatly zero, so that the formalism of the engine becomes ill-defined before it freezes. In RES=RAG, with generation switched off, RES alone remains: a static memory lock. This is internally coherent, since the framework itself states that imbalance disrupts the rhythm. The weakness is that a zero-entropy state has no distinguishable contents to retain, so RES should vanish as well; memory without differences is empty, and the "memory lock" is not a stable end state.

5. Discussion
Summary verdict. As a theory of physical time, IGEG is more tenable than RES=RAG. As an account of why experience has rhythm, RES=RAG addresses a question IGEG does not attempt. Neither framework is established, and they are better regarded as complementary than as rivals.
5.1 Basis of the Tenability Advantage of IGEG
The comparative advantage of IGEG derives from five connected properties.
Formal completeness. IGEG states explicit field equations, a stress-energy tensor and a diffeomorphism-invariant action, from which quantitative predictions can in principle be derived and compared with observation. RES=RAG presently specifies a qualitative balance relation from which no calculation follows.
Correspondence with established theory. IGEG reduces to GR when its field is uniform. Given the empirical record of GR, this is the most important consistency property of a modified-gravity proposal, and it is the reason IGEG cannot be dismissed cheaply. RES=RAG has no corresponding limit and therefore cannot be checked against results already established.
Public falsifiability. IGEG can be shown mistaken by a measured signal it forbids, by a matter coupling that fails fifth-force tests, or by a cosmological fit that cannot be achieved. For RES=RAG as a theory of time, a balance can always be redescribed as an imbalance, which leaves the framework largely immunized against refutation.
Metric time. IGEG inherits from GR the dilation of time with mass and velocity. RES=RAG, in its present form, supplies rhythm and order but no means of stating how much time has elapsed.
Terminological stability. The symbols of IGEG retain their meaning across documents, whereas RES and RAG are expanded differently in different sources, so that it is not yet clear which version should be tested.
The counterfactual battery points in the same direction: across the one hundred counterfactual tests IGEG scores 142 of 300 against 87 for RES=RAG, since in most worlds where established physics decides the outcome its answer is definite. The tests also expose where IGEG is brittle: it is contradicted if gravitational waves travel at a speed different from that of light, if Lorentz symmetry is violated, or if its Fisher-metric clock proves circular. A theory that can be damaged in these ways is one that is being exposed to genuine test.
5.2 Limits of the IGEG Advantage
The lead of IGEG derives largely from the fact that it is, to date, an ordinary scalar field added to Einstein's equations. Such fields have been studied for decades, under names such as quintessence, as candidates for dark energy and inflation. What is new in IGEG is the interpretation of the field as "information". So far this interpretation adds meaning without adding predictions: were the word "information" removed and ψ called a generic scalar, the equations would be unchanged.
A second limitation is the freedom to choose V(ψ). A theory in which a single potential can be tuned to account for dark matter, dark energy and inflation is open to the criticism that such a function can be set to explain nearly any dataset. The claim would become compelling only if V were fixed in advance by a principle and the three phenomena then emerged. This has not been shown. A third limitation is provenance: several publication listings and wider claims attributed to the IGEG author could not be independently verified. The mathematics of IGEG stands or falls on its own, but every citation should be checked before it is relied upon.
5.3 Contributions of RES=RAG
The principal contribution of RES=RAG is the proposition that time may be, at root, a matter of order before measure: one distinct event follows another before any interval between them can be specified. Causal-set theory and relational approaches to quantum gravity reflect the same priority, in which "this came before that" is more basic than "this took ten seconds". A framework that begins from ordering therefore has a foothold that the smooth-geometry starting point of IGEG lacks.
RES=RAG also takes the observer seriously. Physics has not settled how a measurement is made or why a single flowing present is experienced. IGEG is silent on both questions, whereas RES=RAG offers testable proxies in neural rhythms, dialogue coherence and criticality in AI systems. No completed, preregistered study of these proxies was identified, but they are feasible in principle with current tools.
5.4 Evidence from the Added Families
The three added families probe regions in which the original matrix was thin. In Family VIII (quantum-gravity phenomenology) IGEG leads by 76 points (86 versus 10), the largest margin of any family, but the lead is largely inherited. A minimally coupled scalar field added to GR is compatible with the effective-field-theory treatment of gravity, with the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, and with the observed absence of Planck-scale photon dispersion [15], and IGEG receives credit for those properties; it scores only 1 on most criteria that require a quantum-gravity construction the sources do not supply. RES=RAG scores above zero on 10 of the sixty criteria, chiefly those concerning discreteness, order structure and quantum clocks.
In Family IX (thermodynamic and information consistency) IGEG leads 88 to 21, because the Fisher-geometric formalism carries known results (monotonicity under coarse-graining, the Cramér-Rao bound, uniqueness of the metric [5, 6]) that the framework could use but, in the sources reviewed, does not invoke. RES=RAG scores on the qualitative idea that retention and novelty are information-like quantities; neither is quantified. Family X (sociological and meta-scientific viability) is the closest contest (53 versus 44). RES=RAG leads on interdisciplinary reach, accessibility, training pathways, ethical framing and policy relevance, whereas IGEG leads on terminological stability, stability of core claims and compatibility with existing data pipelines. Neither framework satisfies the research-programme criterion of Lakatos [17]: neither has yet generated a corroborated novel prediction.
5.5 Interpretive Confidence
The ranking of the two frameworks as physical theories of time is robust to random perturbation of the scores, to generosity toward RES=RAG, and to symmetric severity. It is not robust to a uniform one-sided downgrading of every IGEG score, which reverses the totals narrowly (Section 4.6). Confidence in the ranking is therefore moderate to high, and confidence that IGEG is correct is considerably lower: most scalar-field extensions of gravity are eventually excluded or confined to a small region of parameter space. IGEG is best characterized as a respectable question awaiting its numbers, and RES=RAG as an intriguing intuition awaiting its mathematics.
6. Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT)
Status of this section. The framework presented here, Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT), is a proposed synthesis constructed by the authors from the two frameworks and from established results of information geometry and quantum foundations. It is not stated in the source texts of either framework. No quantity in it has been derived numerically or tested empirically, and each identification is a postulate until the derivations of Section 6.8 are completed. The section replaces earlier heuristic summation schemes in which the relation between information time and proper time was assumed without being stated as an identity.
6.1 Rationale and Design Requirements
The evaluation of Sections 4 and 5 isolates two defects that block a merger of the frameworks. First, the computational-engine reading of IGEG is circular: the natural-gradient flow of Eq. (5) requires an evolution parameter τ, so the engine presupposes the time it purports to generate (criterion 28; Table 4), and ψ is a posited primitive rather than a derived quantity. Second, RES=RAG supplies an order of events but no measure, so no amount of elapsed time can be stated and no route to relativity exists (criterion 70; Table 4). IGRT is accordingly required (R1) to define time without a pre-existing evolution parameter, (R2) to derive ψ rather than posit it, (R3) to supply a measure of elapsed time that reduces to proper time, and (R4) to yield a measurable signature.
6.2 The Core Identification
Let θ(λ) be a path on a statistical manifold with Fisher metric g_ij (Eq. (4)), traced by the successive RAG events of a system, and let x^μ (λ) be a timelike worldline in spacetime of signature (−, +, +, +). IGRT postulates that the elapsed time along the path is the Fisher-Rao length of the path and, equivalently, the proper time along the worldline:
Δτ=∫√(g_ij θ ̇^i θ ̇^j ) dλ ≡ ∫√(-g_μν x ̇^μ x ̇^ν ) dλ(10)
Here λ is an arbitrary monotonic parameter with no physical meaning, and a dot denotes d/dλ. Each integrand is homogeneous of degree one in the velocities, so that Δτ is invariant under reparametrization of λ: it depends on the path and not on how the path is traversed. The left-hand side is the number of distinguishable states traversed, which is the measure that RES=RAG lacks; the right-hand side is the proper time of general relativity, which is the measure that IGEG inherits. For a discrete sequence of N RAG events the identification takes the form
Δτ_N=∑_(k=1)^N▒√(g_ij (θ_k ) Δθ_k^i Δθ_k^j ) → Δτ (N→∞)(11)
in which each term is the Fisher distance of one event, and the sum approaches Eq. (10) as the events become dense. Eq. (10) is written in units in which one unit of Fisher length corresponds to one unit of proper time and c = 1. Restoring units requires a calibration constant l_I with dimensions of time per unit Fisher length. The identification is a postulate and not a derivation, and the value of the constant is not fixed by it. For a pure state evolving unitarily under a Hamiltonian with energy uncertainty ΔE, the Fubini-Study (quantum Fisher) speed is constant and equal to [3, 4]
(ds_FS)/dt=(2 ΔE)/ℏ(12)
so that l_I=ℏ/(2ΔE) for such a clock. The identification is therefore consistent with ordinary quantum evolution only if the clock states are chosen with a fixed energy uncertainty, or if the metric g_ij is normalized to absorb it. Whether a single universal constant can hold across clocks is the principal open question of the framework (Section 6.7).
6.3 Resolution of the Circularity of IGEG: ψ as an Emergent Quantity
IGRT removes the circularity in two steps. The first concerns the parameter. In Eq. (10) the quantity λ is a gauge label, and τ appears only as a functional of the path. The natural-gradient flow of Eq. (5) is then read as specifying an orbit, that is, which states follow which, and not a flow in a pre-existing time. Ordering is supplied by the sequence of events along the orbit, and measure by the Fisher length of the orbit. The construction parallels the derivation of time from orbit length in timeless formulations of mechanics [11] and the entropic-dynamics programme [12], and it removes the dependence of the engine reading on an external τ.
The second step concerns the origin of time itself. A global state that satisfies a constraint of Wheeler-DeWitt type does not evolve [1]. In the Page-Wootters construction [2] a stationary global state |Ψ⟩⟩ of a clock C and a system S obeys
(H ̂_C+H ̂_S )|Ψ⟩⟩=0(13)
and the state of S conditioned on the clock reading t, which exists only because C and S are entangled, evolves by the ordinary Schrödinger equation:
|φ_S (t)⟩=⟨t|_C |Ψ⟩⟩,  iℏ ∂_t | φ_S (t)⟩=H ̂_S |φ_S (t)⟩(14)
Time is thereby a correlation between subsystems and not a background parameter. In IGRT the Fisher-Rao (Fubini-Study) length of the conditional path t↦|φ_S (t)⟩ is the quantity Δτ of Eq. (10). The scalar field is then defined from the same entanglement structure. Writing ρ_A for the reduced state of a small region A, σ_A for the vacuum reduced state and S for the von Neumann entropy, IGRT proposes
ψ^2 (x)=κ_ψ lim┬(A→x)  (S(ρ_A )-S(σ_A ))/Vol(A) (15)
where κ_ψ is a constant that fixes the dimensions of ψ. The field ψ of IGEG is accordingly a coarse-grained descriptor of the vacuum-subtracted entanglement density and not an independent primitive. This is consistent with the lineage of IGEG: gravity as an equation of state of entanglement [7, 8], and the equality of quantum Fisher information with canonical energy in holographic gravity [9]. Fisher geometry and entanglement are also linked at the level of the metric, because relative entropy is, to second order, one half of the Fisher line element [5]:
D(p_(θ+dθ) ‖ p_θ )=1/2 g_ij dθ^i dθ^j+O(|dθ|^3 )(16)
Gradients of ψ and accumulated Fisher length are therefore two faces of one relation, and ψ and g_ij are not independent inputs. The circularity of IGEG is resolved in the sense that neither ψ nor τ is posited: both are derived from the entanglement structure of the global state. Eq. (15) is schematic. Entanglement entropy is ultraviolet divergent, vacuum subtraction removes only the leading divergence, and regulator dependence remains (Section 6.7).
6.4 Resolution of the Absence of a Metric in RES=RAG
RES=RAG provides discrete, ordered generative events but no measure of the interval between them (criterion 70; Table 4). IGRT assigns to each RAG event the finite displacement Δθ^i on the statistical manifold, so that its Fisher distance is its contribution to elapsed time (Eq. (11)). RES, the retained structure, is the accumulated path, that is, the record of distinguishable states already traversed. Equating Fisher distance with proper time in Eq. (10) then supplies the metric whose absence blocked the connection of RES=RAG to relativity. Relativistic time dilation is inherited from the right-hand side of Eq. (10): a clock that accumulates less proper time per unit of coordinate time executes correspondingly fewer RAG events in that interval. The ordering that RES=RAG places first is preserved, because the sequence of events is the sequence of points along the path, and the measure is added on top of it. The requirement that RES and RAG belong to a mind is also relaxed, because the statistical manifold may describe the states of any system; the observer-inclusive reading is retained as an optional layer and not a premise.
6.5 Rhythm and Observable Signature
The toy model of Section 2.3 is recovered at small displacement. Setting
ψ=ψ_0+x(17)
where ψ_0 is the value at the minimum of V, makes balanced RES=RAG correspond to a uniform field (∇_μ ψ=0), for which Eq. (1) reduces to Einstein gravity, and imbalance correspond to a gradient that sources additional curvature. Neglecting spatial gradients and cosmic expansion, the displacement obeys Eq. (8) with
ω^2=V″(ψ_0 )(18)
so that the tempo of the rhythm is set by the curvature of the potential. An oscillating ultralight scalar field makes the gravitational potential oscillate at twice the field frequency when V is quadratic, and imprints a periodic modulation on pulsar-timing residuals [10]:
f_PTA=2ω/2π=ω/π(19)
A coupling of the field to the constituents of a clock produces, in addition, a fractional frequency modulation in precision clock comparisons,
δν/ν (t)=ε x_0 cos(ωt+φ)(20)
where x_0 is the oscillation amplitude and ε is a dimensionless coupling. In IGRT the coupling is fixed in principle by Eq. (15) once κ_ψ is known; at present it is unspecified. A search that finds a pulsar-timing line and a clock-comparison line must find them in the frequency ratio of two, in accordance with Eqs. (19) and (20). The ratio is generic to ultralight scalar fields and therefore tests the oscillation core of the synthesis and not the identification of Eq. (10); a test specific to the identification requires the derivation of ε.

6.7 Failure Modes and Open Problems
* Calibration constant. The constant l_I relating Fisher length to time depends on the energy uncertainty of the clock state (Eq. (12)). If it cannot be made universal, Eq. (10) fails as an identity and survives at most as a clock-specific relation.
* Signature. The Fisher metric is positive-definite, whereas spacetime has Lorentzian signature. Eq. (10) equates lengths along curves and does not identify the two tensors. The spacelike sector requires additional structure that is not supplied here.
* Definition of ψ. Eq. (15) depends on a regulator and on the constant κ_ψ. A scheme-independent definition, with units and a measurable counterpart, is required.
* Matter coupling. The coupling of ψ to ordinary matter is unspecified in IGEG, and the clock coupling ε of Eq. (20) cannot be computed without it.
* Choice of clock. Page-Wootters constructions depend on the choice of clock subsystem, and their extension to interacting clocks and multi-time correlations remains debated.
* Choice of statistical manifold. The family p(x∣θ) on which g_ij is defined must be specified for each class of system, and the Fisher metric becomes degenerate for zero-entropy states.
* Differing priorities. IGEG is physics-first; RES=RAG, in the sources reviewed, is relation- and consciousness-first. A merger demotes one of these priorities.
6.8 Research Program for Numerical Derivation
As a proposed framework, IGRT requires the following steps before it can be promoted from hypothesis to theory.
1.	Fix a glossary for RES and RAG, with symbols, so that the terms cease to drift between papers.
2.	Define ψ through Eq. (15) with a stated regulator, units and κ_ψ, and specify its coupling to matter.
3.	Derive Eq. (10) for ordinary clocks, first for a two-level atomic clock in a weak gravitational field, and determine whether l_I can be made universal.
4.	Compute ω from V″ for a chosen potential, and state the resulting signals of Eqs. (19) and (20) in pulsar-timing and optical-clock data.
5.	Run the cognitive proxies of RES=RAG as preregistered experiments, so that the RES=RAG side also risks failure.
6.	Score IGRT itself with the 500-criterion matrix, using the multi-rater protocol of Section 3.7.
6.9 Assessment of the Synthesis
At the level of ideas, the two frameworks fit well and the fit is not forced. At the level of mathematics, IGRT provides a credible route through the Fisher-Rao path length, the entanglement definition of ψ and the oscillation of the potential minimum, but the route exists on paper only. If Eq. (10) were derived, the IGEG attack on the engine clock would move from wounded to open, and the RES=RAG attack on the absence of a metric from failed to wounded (Table 4). At the level of experiment, the shared test is the ultralight-scalar signature of Section 6.5, which is generic and does not yet isolate the identification. The frameworks are therefore compatible but not yet unified. A successful synthesis would reduce neither RES=RAG to a footnote of IGEG nor the reverse; it would allow each to do what it does best, joined by one precise equation.
7. Conclusions
A 500-criterion adversarial evaluation, supplemented by twenty red-team attacks, one hundred counterfactual tests and three observational discriminators, finds IGEG the more tenable of the two frameworks as a physical theory of time (712/1500 against 264/1500), principally because it possesses explicit field equations, a correspondence limit to general relativity, public falsifiability and inherited metric time. RES=RAG remains competitive on observer-dependent, ordering and cognitive criteria, which lie outside the scope of IGEG, and it narrows the gap in the sociological and meta-scientific family. The ranking is robust to random scoring error and to generosity toward RES=RAG, but is sensitive to a uniform one-sided downgrading of IGEG, and it rests on single-rater scoring whose reliability has yet to be tested. Neither framework is empirically established.
The proposed Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT) framework identifies the Fisher-Rao length of a path of retention-generation events with proper time (Eq. (10)) and derives ψ from entanglement (Eq. (15)). It is intended to resolve the circularity of the IGEG engine reading and the absence of a metric in RES=RAG, and it remains a proposed framework requiring numerical derivation. Its principal empirical implication, a periodic modulation of pulsar-timing and atomic-clock data by an ultralight scalar oscillation, offers a concrete route to testing it. Priorities for future work are the multi-rater reliability study of Section 3.7, the derivation of Eq. (10) and the universality of its calibration constant, the specification of the matter coupling of ψ, and preregistered tests of the cognitive proxies of RES=RAG.

Appendix B. Glossary
Table B1. Glossary of technical terms.
TermDefinitionActionA functional of the fields from which a theory's equations of motion follow by the variational principle.Scalar fieldA field that assigns a single number to each point of spacetime.Fisher metricA Riemannian metric on a statistical manifold that quantifies the distinguishability of nearby probability distributions.Fisher-Rao distanceThe geodesic length, under the Fisher metric, between two points of a statistical manifold; it counts distinguishable states along a path.GradientThe local rate and direction of change of a quantity with respect to position.Wheeler-DeWitt equationThe Hamiltonian-constraint equation of canonical quantum gravity, whose solutions do not evolve with respect to any external time, giving rise to the frozen-formalism problem of time.No-hair theoremThe result that, under standard assumptions, stationary black holes in GR are characterized by mass, spin and charge alone and cannot carry additional hair such as a simple scalar field.FalsifiableCapable, at least in principle, of being refuted by observation.TenabilityThe degree to which a framework can be held without internal contradiction while being exposed to formal or empirical refutation.Ringdown echoA hypothetical late-time repetition of the gravitational-wave signal following a compact-object merger, which would indicate a reflective structure in place of an event horizon.Equivalence principleThe local indistinguishability of gravitation and acceleration; the universality of free fall.Fifth forceA hypothetical interaction beyond the four known fundamental forces, the strength of which is constrained experimentally.CriticalityA regime poised between order and disorder in which small perturbations may have large effects.ImmunizationThe reformulation of a theory such that no observation could contradict it, rendering it unfalsifiable.Inter-rater reliability (IRR)The degree of agreement among independent raters scoring the same items, typically quantified by statistics such as Krippendorff's α or Cohen's κ.Information-Geometric Resonant Time (IGRT)The proposed synthesis in which elapsed time is the Fisher-Rao length of a path of retention-generation events, identified with proper time (Eq. 10), and in which ψ is derived from entanglement.Proper timeThe time measured by a clock along its own worldline, given by the integral of the square root of minus the metric contracted with the four-velocity.Relative entropyA non-negative measure of the distinguishability of one probability distribution or quantum state from another; to second order it equals one half of the Fisher line element.Entanglement entropyThe von Neumann entropy of the reduced state of a region, which quantifies the quantum correlations between the region and its complement.Fubini-Study metricThe Fisher metric of pure quantum states; its line element is the quantum Fisher information of the state family.Page-Wootters mechanismA construction in which a stationary global state of a clock and a system yields, upon conditioning on the clock reading, ordinary unitary evolution of the system.Pulsar-timing arrayA set of millisecond pulsars whose arrival-time residuals are searched for correlated signals, including the oscillation of the gravitational potential by an ultralight scalar field.Landauer boundThe minimum heat dissipated by the erasure of one bit of information, kT ln 2.Research programme (Lakatos)A sequence of theories sharing a hard core; it is progressive if it predicts novel facts that are corroborated and degenerating if it merely accommodates known facts.Planck scaleThe scale of length, time and energy at which quantum-gravity effects are expected to become significant.
